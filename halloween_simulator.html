How does this little world work?

The places & the people

Guests join a costume party in Costume House, which has room for 20 people at a time. Pumpkin House has room for 24. Guests wait in arrival order when a house is full. Each guest visits both houses, starting with either one, then walks to the unlimited Picnic Area.

Doors open at 6:00 PM. New arrivals stop at 7:30 PM; house activities finish at 8:00 PM. People who run out of time leave without finishing both. Resting ends at 8:30 PM, followed by the walk to the exit.

Near closing, an activity starts only if the guest can finish it in time. A queue may still be waiting even when some spaces are empty.

Why the map matters

Paths have different lengths and people walk at different speeds. Travel time changes when they reach a queue and whether they finish before closing. Each face is one guest replaying that exact journey. A face changes color when it walks, waits, visits a house, or rests. A long wait or missing an activity can turn a smile into a sad face. The ring follows the next guest entering from the moment you turn following on. When that guest leaves, it switches to the next new arrival. If no more guests will arrive, the ring disappears. Local positions inside houses and queue rows are schematic.

One choice, everything else fixed

Up to 400 guests per night, with 8 staff: 2 at the entrance, 2 at the costume house, 3 at the pumpkin house, and 1 at the picnic area. Entrance check-in is immediate in this model.

The nightly fixed cost is $1,800: $600 staff, $800 pumpkin materials, and $400 setup. All materials are included. Profit = ticket income − $1,800. Unfinished activities do not receive refunds.

Compare your prices

Average profit gives every night equal weight. The average completion rate is the total number of admitted guests who finish both houses divided by the total admitted guests across the same 1,000 nights. Each guest has equal weight. Both activities means Costume House and Pumpkin House. The “out of 100” description expresses this proportion; it is not a count of 100 people on the animation. The example night can have more or fewer than 100 guests. With no admitted guests, the rate is unavailable (—). Displayed percentages are rounded down to one decimal place. For a whole-percent minimum, a displayed percentage at or above that minimum also meets the exact minimum; equality counts. For every price, the model calculates the same 1,000 simulated nights and their exact average profit. It selects the actual simulated night whose profit is closest to that average; if nights tie, it selects the first. The animation shows that night’s own guests and queues, not average queue lengths. A different price may select a different example. Weather and guests vary across these saved scenarios. These are fictional classroom assumptions and schematic locations, not real park data. There is no crowd congestion on the paths.
