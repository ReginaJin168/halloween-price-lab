How does this little world work?

The places & the people

Haunted House has room for 20 people at a time. Pumpkin House has room for 24. Guests wait in arrival order when a house is full. Each guest visits both houses, in a randomly chosen order, then walks to the unlimited Picnic Area.

Doors open at 6:00 PM. New arrivals stop at 7:30 PM; house activities finish at 8:00 PM. People who run out of time leave without finishing both. Resting ends at 8:30 PM, followed by the walk to the exit.

Near closing, an activity starts only if the guest can finish it in time. A queue may still be waiting even when some spaces are empty.

Why the map matters

Paths have different lengths and people walk at different speeds. Travel time changes when they reach a queue and whether they finish before closing. Faces replay those exact simulated journeys. Local positions inside houses and queue rows are schematic. A smile means the guest’s current experience is at least 90; a crying face means it is below 90. Waiting points are deducted as the wait happens. Unfinished-activity points are deducted when the houses close, not when a guest first arrives. Seeking backward also restores the earlier expressions.

One choice, everything else fixed

Up to 400 guests per night, with 8 staff: 2 at the entrance, 2 at the haunted house, 3 at the pumpkin house, and 1 at the picnic area. Entrance check-in is immediate in this model.

The nightly fixed cost is $1,800: $600 staff, $800 pumpkin materials, and $400 setup. All materials are included. Profit = ticket income − $1,800. Unfinished activities do not receive refunds.

What counts as a good experience?

Each guest starts at 100. Subtract 0.8 points per minute in line and 25 points for each unfinished activity; the score never falls below zero. It covers admitted guests and does not score how expensive a ticket feels. With no guests, experience is N/A.

Average profit gives every night equal weight. Average experience gives every admitted guest equal weight. “Best tested” means the highest average profit among prices you tried, not the highest experience.

Weather and guests vary from night to night. “Another night” changes only the single-night replay; the 1,000-night comparison stays the same. These are fictional classroom assumptions and schematic locations, not real park data. There is no crowd congestion on the paths.

What does “90” mean?

Classroom satisfaction = 100 minus 0.8 points per minute waiting, minus 25 per unfinished activity, with a minimum of 0. It does not measure ticket affordability or real survey opinions.

All $1–$100 whole-dollar prices face the same 1,000 simulated nights (classroom-2026). Profit is averaged per night; satisfaction across admitted guests. No guests means N/A. The rule uses the unrounded average, not a guarantee that every guest or night scores 90. The trophy is best within these tested prices.
